The problem

Church questions deserve answers you can check.

A question about faith is not a trivia question. When someone asks in a hard week, the answer should come from somewhere they can open, read and trust — not from a confident voice that never shows its source.

No source, no trust

An answer without a verse is an opinion in a robe. If you cannot see where a claim comes from, you cannot weigh it — you can only take it on trust.

A confident guess is still a guess

Most chat tools answer every question, even when they have nothing solid to stand on. In matters of faith, a made-up answer is worse than no answer at all.

A pastor should be able to audit it

A small set of passages that a church leader can read in one sitting beats a large model no congregation can verify. Small and checkable is the point.

How it works

Three rules, kept in the open.

1

43 passages. Nothing else.

The whole corpus is 43 passages from the King James Version, in the public domain, bundled inside the app. There is no other source to draw from.

2

Every claim carries its citation.

Each answer quotes the passage it rests on, word for word, and wears a confidence badge — High, Medium or Low — so you can see how firmly it is grounded.

3

If nothing fits, it says so.

When no passage speaks to a question, Grace Notes answers “not found in the provided passages” — honestly, rather than dressing up a guess as doctrine.

Honest by design

No backend. No model call. No network. The corpus and the retrieval run entirely in your browser, and nothing you type leaves your device. A human stays in the loop — every answer is meant to be checked against its cited verse.
